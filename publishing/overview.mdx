Summer Engine is the game engine built for AI. You can also export your game as your own builds and release them on other stores or your own website. This section shows how, step by step for each platform and store.

<Note>
  New to game publishing? Start with the **macOS** or **Windows** export, then publish to **itch.io** for your first release. It is the shortest path to players.
</Note>

## Platforms

| Platform | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Windows** | The largest PC gaming market. See [Windows export](/publishing/windows-export). |
| **macOS** | Intel and Apple Silicon. See [macOS export](/publishing/macos-export). |
| **Linux** | Also runs on Steam Deck. |
| **Web** | Plays in the browser, for example on itch.io or your own site. |
| **Android and iOS** | Store releases need each store's own developer account and signing. On Summer Games, the Summer Games apps run your game with no account of your own: see [Export your game](/publishing/export). |
| **Consoles** | Summer has no console export of its own. See [Console publishing](/publishing/consoles). |

Web, Linux, Android and iOS builds need export templates. See [Export templates](/publishing/export#export-templates).

## Before you start

### 1. Turn on texture compression (once per project)

Turn on the texture formats for your target platforms. You do this once per project.

**Go to**: Project → Project Settings → Rendering → Textures → VRAM Compression

| Publishing to | Turn on | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Mac, iOS, Android** | Import ETC2 ASTC | Apple Silicon and mobile GPUs use this format |
| **Windows, Linux** | Import S3TC BPTC | PC graphics cards use this format |
| **Mac and Windows** | Both formats | The common setup for desktop releases |

<Tip>
  **Releasing on Steam for both Mac and Windows?** Turn on both ETC2 ASTC and S3TC BPTC at the same time, so Summer reimports your textures only once.
</Tip>

**What happens when you turn them on?**
- Summer reimports your textures (1-5 minutes, depending on project size).
- The imported files take more disk space, because each texture is stored in each format.
- Your game runs on those platforms without texture errors.

### 2. Test your game first

Before you export, make sure your game works in the editor:
- Play through your game from start to finish.
- Test every major feature.
- Fix any errors in the Output panel.

Export does not fix bugs. It packages your game as it is.

## What each store asks of you

### itch.io
- No approval process. Upload and publish in minutes.
- You choose itch.io's share of each sale (open revenue sharing: 10% by default, 0% allowed).
- Good for playtests, game jams and web games.
- A smaller audience than Steam.

### Steam
- The largest PC gaming audience, with achievements, cloud saves and Workshop.
- A Steam Direct fee per game, returned after your game earns enough.
- Valve reviews your store page and build before release.
- Valve takes 30% of sales, less above high revenue thresholds.

### Direct download (your website)
- You keep the revenue, minus your payment provider's fees.
- You control distribution.
- Code signing avoids security warnings.
- You build your own update and payment flow.

## Common questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I need export templates?">
    **Sometimes.** The Mac app of Summer Engine comes with the Mac template, and the Windows app with the Windows templates. Web, Linux, Android and iOS builds need templates from Summer's download server.

    Your agent installs them with `summer_export_templates`. Do not use the editor's own template download: it fetches stock templates, not Summer's. See [Export templates](/publishing/export#export-templates).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I export to multiple platforms from one project?">
    **Yes.** Turn on both ETC2 ASTC and S3TC BPTC, then make one export preset per platform.

    You can export Mac, Windows and Linux builds from the same project.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I need to code sign my game?">
    **It depends on where you release:**

    - **Steam**: usually not. Games that Steam installs do not get the download warning. Signing is still good practice.
    - **itch.io**: not required. Players see a security warning they can click through.
    - **Direct download, Mac**: yes, or players see "unidentified developer" warnings.
    - **Direct download, Windows**: recommended, or players see "unknown publisher" warnings.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How big will my exported game be?">
    It depends mostly on your assets: textures, audio and 3D models. A small 2D game is often under 150 MB. A 3D game with many textures can reach several GB.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I export for consoles (Switch, PlayStation, Xbox)?">
    Not directly. Summer has no console export of its own. Console versions need approval from the platform holder, its SDK and dev kits, and certification. See the [Console publishing guide](/publishing/consoles).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
