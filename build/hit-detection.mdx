On every connection, what a player sees is a fraction of a second old. If the
server only checked where everyone is *now*, a perfectly aimed shot could
miss because the target has moved on since. Summer keeps a short history of
where every character was. When a player fires, the server checks the shot
against the moment the player saw, so if it was on target on their screen,
it counts. This works for 3D games.

- **Hits match the screen.** A shot that was on target when the player fired
  counts, even though the target kept moving while the shot travelled to the
  server.
- **Walls still block.** The server checks the walls too, so nobody gets shot
  through solid cover.
- **No faked hits.** The attacking player's game sends only what they did,
  such as the aim and the weapon. The server works out who was hit, so a
  modified client can't claim hits it didn't make.

## Pick your setup

### Instant shots

The shot travels in a straight line the moment the player fires, like a
rifle or a laser. Summer checks the line against every other character's hit
shape and the walls, at the moment the shooter saw. Your server decides what
a hit does: damage, a kill, a hit marker for the shooter.

### Abilities, swings and projectiles

Not every attack is a straight line. A shotgun spreads in a cone, a sword
sweeps an arc, a fireball travels along a path, and a ground slam hits an
area. Your game decides the shape, its range and who it can hurt. Summer
gives your server the exact moment the player saw, with every character
where it was on their screen, so you test your shape against that.

## How far back the server looks

Your game chooses how old a player's view may be and still count. Summer
recommends a quarter of a second, which covers players with round trips up
to about 170 ms. A player whose view is older than that has their shot
refused instead of counted.

## Testing it on your machine

Local Play runs your server and several game windows on one computer, and it
can add delay, jitter and packet loss to every window. Shoot at a moving
player with a delay of around 100 ms: the hit should count when your
crosshair was on them. See
[Make your game multiplayer](/build/multiplayer#testing-it-on-your-machine).
