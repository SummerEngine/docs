A party is a group of friends who want to play together. Players make parties
in the Summer app: they invite friends, chat, and pick a leader. Your game sees
who is in the party, lets them choose things together, and brings the whole
party into the same match when the leader presses **Play**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Party up">
    Players invite friends to a party. Your game can open Summer's invite
    screen from its own menu, so nobody has to leave the game.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose together">
    The leader picks things for the whole party, such as the mode or the map.
    Each member picks their own, such as a character or "ready". Everyone in
    the party sees the same choices.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Play together">
    The leader presses Play. Everyone in the party who has your game open
    follows into the same search and the same match. On a
    [team queue](/build/matchmaking#fixed-teams-2v2-3v3-5v5), the party always
    lands on the same team.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Summer runs the party itself, and your game builds the experience around it.

## Pick your setup

### Play together from the menu

The leader's **Play** button starts one search for the whole party, and every
member follows it automatically. Members see who leads and that they're
waiting for them. A player who isn't in a party plays solo with the same Play
button.

<Prompt description="**Let parties play together.** The leader presses Play and every member follows into the same match.">
In my Summer project, let players who are in a Summer party play together. Use
the Summer `summer-parties` skill, and the `summer-matchmaking` skill for my
queue; if you don't have them installed, find them in the Summer library.

- The party leader's Play button searches for the whole party, and every
  member follows into the same match.
- Members see who leads the party and that they're waiting for them.
- A player who isn't in a party still plays solo with the same Play button.

Read my project first and fit this into my existing menus. Then test the solo
path with Local Play, and tell me what I can only check once the game is
hosted.
</Prompt>

### Choose things together before the match

Give the party a small shared screen before they play. The leader's choices,
such as the mode or the map, show up for everyone. Each member's own choices,
such as their character or a Ready check, show up next to their name.

### Invite friends from your game

Add an **Invite friends** button that opens Summer's own invite screen, and a
**Party** button for leaving, changing the leader or removing a member. Summer
handles the invites and the party chat, so your game doesn't have to.

<Prompt description="**Add Invite and Party buttons** that open Summer's party screens.">
In my Summer project, add an Invite friends button and a Party button to my
menu. Use the Summer `summer-parties` skill; if you don't have it installed,
find it in the Summer library.

- Invite friends opens Summer's invite screen.
- Party opens Summer's party screen for leaving, changing the leader and
  removing members.
- Show the buttons only where parties are offered, and keep the menu tidy
  when they aren't.

Read my project first and match the style of my existing buttons.
</Prompt>

## Testing it on your machine

Parties belong to players' Summer accounts, so Local Play has no parties:
every test window plays solo through the normal Play button. Build and test
that solo path first. The party flow, with the leader searching and members
following, shows up once the game is hosted and you play with real friends.

Guest players, and younger players who aren't offered social features, also
play solo, so the solo path matters for them too.
