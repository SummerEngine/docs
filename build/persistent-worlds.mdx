Most matches end and are gone. A **persistent World** is a place players come
back to, such as a shared base, a farm or a realm. Your game's server saves it
while people play. When nobody is playing, Summer can let the World sleep, and
the next time someone joins, it wakes up and restores from its last save.

## What your players get

<img className="block dark:hidden" src="/images/guide-art/persistent-worlds/lifecycle-light.svg" alt="Four steps for one World: players play and build, the World is saved at checkpoints, it sleeps with no server running while nobody plays, and it wakes up restored from its save when a player returns." />
<img className="hidden dark:block" src="/images/guide-art/persistent-worlds/lifecycle-dark.svg" alt="Four steps for one World: players play and build, the World is saved at checkpoints, it sleeps with no server running while nobody plays, and it wakes up restored from its save when a player returns." />

- **The world as they left it.** Walls they built, chests they filled and crops
  they planted are all still there.
- **Their own Worlds, one tap away.** Players find the Worlds they've played
  under **My Worlds** in Summer Games, and your game can offer a "Return to my
  World" button.
- **A warning before planned restarts.** When a World is about to restart or
  sleep for maintenance, your game can tell players in advance, and the World
  saves before it stops.
- **Worlds that survive updates.** Publishing a new version of your game
  never touches a World's saves. Today an existing private World keeps running
  the version it started on, and new Worlds use your newest version. Coming
  soon, Worlds move to your newest version when it can read their saves: see
  [Changing your save format](#changing-your-save-format).

## What to save where

A World save holds what belongs to the **place**. What belongs to a **player**,
such as their level or the coins in their pocket, goes in
[player progress](/build/player-progress), so it follows them to every World.

<img className="block dark:hidden" src="/images/guide-art/persistent-worlds/what-goes-where-light.svg" alt="The World save belongs to the place: walls, chests and crops. Player progress follows the player to every World: level, unlocks, coins and settings." />
<img className="hidden dark:block" src="/images/guide-art/persistent-worlds/what-goes-where-dark.svg" alt="The World save belongs to the place: walls, chests and crops. Player progress follows the player to every World: level, unlocks, coins and settings." />

Your game decides what goes in a save, and the save is always one consistent
moment. If a player moves an item from a chest into their inventory, the chest
and the inventory are saved together.

## Pick your setup

### A private World for a group

Each group gets its own World that only its players return to, like a Realm
or a co-op farm. Players come back to it from **My Worlds** or from your
game's menu, and a sleeping World wakes up and restores before they're let in.

### Shared Worlds anyone can join

Players who search for a game join an existing World that has a free seat,
waking it up if it's asleep. Summer starts a new World only when none has
room. Good for public servers that grow a community over time.

### Warn players before a planned restart

Before Summer restarts a World or puts it to sleep for maintenance, your game
gets notice in advance. Use it to warn players and wrap up anything in
progress. Gameplay then pauses for a final save, and the World comes back
exactly as it was.

<img className="block dark:hidden" src="/images/guide-art/persistent-worlds/shutdown-light.svg" alt="A timeline: the World gets a notice, the game warns players that the server restarts in 5 minutes, gameplay pauses for a final save, and later the same World comes back restored." />
<img className="hidden dark:block" src="/images/guide-art/persistent-worlds/shutdown-dark.svg" alt="A timeline: the World gets a notice, the game warns players that the server restarts in 5 minutes, gameplay pauses for a final save, and later the same World comes back restored." />

<Prompt description="**Warn players before a restart** and make the final save.">
In my Summer project, warn players before my persistent World restarts or
sleeps, and save it before it stops. Use the Summer `summer-world-saves`
skill; if you don't have it installed, find it in the Summer library.

- When a planned stop is announced, show players a countdown, such as
  "Server restarts in 5 minutes", and hide it again if the stop is cancelled.
- Don't start long activities, like a boss fight, close to the stop.
- Make the final save when Summer asks for it.

Keep the regular saves too: an unexpected crash doesn't send a warning.
</Prompt>

## Changing your save format

<Note>
  **Coming soon.** Everything in this section needs a Summer Engine release
  with save migrations (Engine interface 7) and the matching Summer update.
  Until both ship, a World stays on the version of your game it started on,
  and a new version must keep writing the same save version to restore the
  same World.
</Note>

Your save format changes as your game grows: a crop gets a growth stage, a
chest gets more slots, an item is renamed. Every save is written in a **save
version**, a name you choose such as `farm.v2`. When your new version of the
game writes a new save version, it also says which older ones it can still
read, and your game turns old saves into the new format.

### How a save is migrated

1. **List every save version this version of your game reads**, oldest first,
   in your World definition. The last one is the save version it writes.

   ```json
   "persistence": {
     "mode": "checkpoint",
     "recovery_point_seconds": 60,
     "snapshot_schemas": ["farm.v1", "farm.v2", "farm.v3"],
     "migration_ids": []
   }
   ```

2. **Write one migration step per older save version.** A step takes the
   bytes of one save version and returns the bytes of the next one in the
   list. Summer Engine chains the steps when a World loads, so a `farm.v1`
   save goes `farm.v1` → `farm.v2` → `farm.v3`. You never write a step that
   skips a version.
3. **Keep the old steps.** A World that has been asleep for months may still
   be on your oldest save version.

Summer never changes your save bytes; only your game's code does. Every
earlier save stays in the World's history, so a failed migration loses
nothing.

### When Worlds move to your new version

Choose an upgrade policy for your game:

| Policy | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Automatically on next visit** (default) | When a sleeping World next wakes, it moves to your newest version if that version can read its save. |
| **When I choose** | Worlds move only when you upgrade one World or press **Upgrade all now**. |
| **Never** | Worlds keep running the version they're on. |

- You can also **pin** a single World so it never moves, whatever the policy.
- Automatic upgrades only ever go to your newest published version. You can
  move a World to any other version that reads its save yourself.
- A World whose save your newest version can't read stays on its current
  version and keeps working. It isn't stuck: the version that wrote a save can
  always read it.

### Every upgrade is a trial

When a World upgrades, the new version loads the save, migrates it and saves
the result **before any player is let in**. A player who joins meanwhile sees
the World waking up and gets in once the upgrade is saved.

If the migration fails, the server crashes before that first save, or the
upgrade takes longer than 5 minutes, the World goes back to the version and
save it had before, untouched, and sleeps. On your game's **Saved Worlds**
page it shows **Upgrade failed** with the reason, for example "Version 13's
save migration from farm.v1 failed: farm.v1 save is not a dictionary".

- A failure caused by your game is not tried again automatically for that
  World. Fix the step and choose **Retry**, or publish a newer version.
- A failure caused by Summer, such as a server machine going away, doesn't
  count against your version. The World tries again on its next visit.

Each World on **Saved Worlds** shows one status: *Up to date*, *Upgrades on
next visit*, *Upgrading…*, *Upgrade failed*, *Pinned*, or *Can't upgrade*
(your newest version can't read its save). A failed World offers **Retry**,
**Pin this World**, **Run on another version** and **Start fresh**. Starting
fresh asks you to type the World's name, and keeps the old saves as history.

### Check your migrations before you publish

Keep a few real saves of each older save version in your project, under
`res://summer/save_fixtures/<save version>/`, for example
`res://summer/save_fixtures/farm.v1/big-farm.save`. Summer Engine can run your
migration steps over them on your machine: every one must migrate to your
current save version and load. Local Play can't show a migration on its own,
because it starts every run with an empty World.

After you publish, Summer runs the same check on its servers with your save
fixtures and up to 20 recent saves from your players' Worlds of each older
save version. Your players' saves never leave Summer's servers. The result
appears next to the release, such as "Save check: farm.v1 → farm.v3 passed on
3 test saves and 20 player saves".

- A failed check only keeps that version from upgrading Worlds automatically.
  It never stops you publishing.
- If Summer couldn't run the check, nothing is held back: each World's upgrade
  is still a trial that rolls back on failure.

### Strict compatibility

Turn on **strict compatibility** in your game's Saved Worlds settings if you
never want to drop an old save version. Publishing is then refused when your
new version stops reading a save version an earlier version wrote, or when
its own save fixtures fail the check. Your newest version can then always
upgrade every World.

It's off by default, so you can drop support for very old save versions.
Worlds on a dropped save version keep running the version that wrote them.

## Testing it on your machine

Local Play runs your persistent World with saves kept in memory. Every run
starts with an empty World, saving works and returns a confirmation, and
nothing is kept after the run ends. That's enough to check that your World
saves while people play and restores before anyone joins. Coming back to the
same World after a real restart happens in the hosted game.
