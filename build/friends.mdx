Players bring their Summer friends into every game. Your game can show which
friends are online and who's playing it right now, and open Summer's own
friends list, profiles and chat on top of the game. If messaging is part of
your game, it can also read and send direct messages. Players stay in
control: your game asks before it reads their friends or messages, and it
keeps working if they say no.

## What your players get

<img className="block dark:hidden" src="/images/guide-art/friends/list-light.svg" alt="A friends list grouped by status: friends in this game first, then online, in another game, and offline." />
<img className="hidden dark:block" src="/images/guide-art/friends/list-dark.svg" alt="A friends list grouped by status: friends in this game first, then online, in another game, and offline." />

- **Friends and what they're doing:** each friend shows as in this game,
  online, in another game, or offline. Another game is never named.
- **Summer's screens over your game:** the friends list, any player's profile
  with add friend, message, invite to party and report, and a chat with a
  friend. Your game opens them with no permission needed.
- **Direct messages inside your game,** when the player allows it.

Friend requests, blocking and reporting all live in the Summer app, so your
game never has to build them.

## Pick your setup

### Summer's screens over your game

The simplest setup needs no permission at all. A Friends button opens
Summer's friends list over your game. Tapping a player on your scoreboard
opens their profile, where the player can add them as a friend, message them,
invite them to a party or report them. This covers what most games need.

### A friends list with who's playing

Your game can show the player's friends in its own style, with friends who
are in this game at the top. Reading the list needs the player's permission:
your game declares that it wants it, and asks when the player opens the list.
Summer shows its own consent screen. If the player says no, the list stays
private and the game plays on as normal.

### Direct messages in your game

When messaging is central to your game, such as a turn-based game where
friends chat between moves, your game can read and send the player's direct
messages. Reading, sending and marking messages as read are each a separate
permission the player grants. Summer filters every message, and players can
only message their friends. For chat with everyone in a match, use
[text chat](/build/chat) instead.

## Testing it on your machine

Local Play has no Summer accounts, so friends features report that they
aren't available. Use it to check that your game hides them cleanly and keeps
working. Summer also keeps players under 13 out of social features, so the
same hidden version covers them. Friends lists, profiles and messages show
real data in a hosted game: test with two accounts that are friends.
