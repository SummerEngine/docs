Summer keeps two kinds of board for your game. A **rating** is a skill number
that moves after every ranked match. A **score board** holds any number your
game counts, such as wins, coins or levels cleared. Players see their own number,
their place, and the top 50 players. Every number comes from your game's
server, never from a player's device, so nobody can fake their way to the top.

## What your players get

<img className="block dark:hidden" src="/images/guide-art/leaderboards/board-light.svg" alt="A leaderboard listing the top players by rating, with the player's own row highlighted, next to the player's rating, its last change and their place." />
<img className="hidden dark:block" src="/images/guide-art/leaderboards/board-dark.svg" alt="A leaderboard listing the top players by rating, with the player's own row highlighted, next to the player's rating, its last change and their place." />

- **Their own number:** their rating or score, and how much it moved in the
  last match.
- **Their place:** for example "#412 of 9,830". Further down a rating board,
  below the top 1,000, the place is a percentage instead, such as "Top 12%".
- **The top 50:** best first, by display name, with the player's own row
  marked when they're in it.

Players can open a board at any time, including from the main menu outside a
match.

## Pick your board

### Skill rating: ranked matches

Every ranked queue keeps a rating for each player. When a match ends, your
server reports who won, lost or drew, and Summer moves everyone's rating with
Elo. Beating a stronger player is worth more than beating a weaker one. It
works for 1v1 and for two teams. New players start at 1200, or at a starting
value you choose. Summer also uses the rating to
[match players of similar skill](/build/matchmaking#ranked-match-players-of-similar-skill).

### Your own rating rules

Elo rates two sides against each other. When that doesn't fit, your server
decides how much each player's rating moves after a match, up to 1,000 points
either way. Use it for co-op modes where a group plays against the game, such
as a dungeon run rated by its difficulty, or for any formula of your own.

failing costs a little"].

</Prompt>

### Score boards: anything your game counts

A score board ranks any number your game keeps track of: total wins, coins
collected, levels cleared, mobs defeated. Your server records the new score
whenever it changes, for example at the end of a match. Higher scores rank
first, and on a tie the player who got there first ranks higher. A score board
doesn't need matchmaking, and a game can have up to 32 of them.

## Testing it on your machine

With Local Play, your server can end a match and get test rating changes
back, so you can build and test the whole end-of-match flow on one computer.
The leaderboards themselves, players' places and score boards only show real
data in a hosted game. Under Local Play they report that they aren't
available, so make sure your screens handle that.
