Cosmetics change how a player looks without changing how the game plays. You
decide where a look can go, such as a head slot or a body slot, and which items
fit there. The player picks one of the items they own. Your game's server checks
that they own it, and then every player in the match sees it, including players
who join later.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick">
    The player chooses from the items they own for each slot your game has,
    such as a head, a body or a weapon.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Checked">
    Your game's server reads the player's inventory and accepts the choice
    only if they own the item and it fits that slot.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Seen">
    Everyone in the match sees the same look, and so does anyone who joins
    later.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Cosmetic items are ordinary items in your game's catalog in Studio. Players
get them by buying them in your [store](/build/store).

## Pick your setup

### Slots and looks

Each slot lists the items that fit it. Each item maps to a look you made in
your game: a **skin** is a material on the character, and a **hat** or another
attachment is a scene you attach, for example to the character's head.

### Keep the loadout between matches

Players expect their chosen looks to still be on next time they play. Save
each player's choices with their progress and put them back on when the player
joins a match.

<Prompt description="**Remember each player's loadout.** Their cosmetics are back on in the next match.">
In my Summer project, remember which cosmetics each player has equipped and
put them back on in their next match. Use the Summer `summer-player-data` and
`summer-store` skills; if you don't have them installed, find them in the
Summer library.

- Save the loadout when the player changes it.
- When the player joins, equip their saved items again, but only those they
  still own.

Read my project first and explain where the loadout is saved and loaded before
you change anything. Then test it with Local Play and tell me what you saw.
</Prompt>

See [Save player progress](/build/player-progress) for how saving works.

## Testing it on your machine

Under Local Play, give your test players cosmetic items in your Local Play test
users file. Each test player then owns those items, and your slots, your
server's check and the looks all work on one computer. The items get local
stand-in names there, so test with the items the player's game actually lists
rather than with your real catalog names.
