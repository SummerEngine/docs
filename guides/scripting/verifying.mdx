A scene script's result tells you what the script *did*. It cannot tell you whether the scene
is now *right*. For that there are two kinds of evidence, and they answer different questions:

- **Structured state** — a world snapshot, a snapshot diff, a scene-tree read, a live read
  from the running game — proves **facts**: which nodes exist, where they are, how big they
  are, what changed.
- **Pixels** — a screenshot of the viewport, a render of the scene, a frame of the running
  game — prove **appearance**: composition, scale to the eye, lighting, whether it reads as a
  forest.

Neither substitutes for the other. A diff can report forty trees added at plausible positions
while the screenshot shows them all untextured; a screenshot can look right while the diff
shows half the trees unowned and gone after the save. Take both, every time.

## The before/after discipline

Around **every** run:

1. **Before:** `summer_world_snapshot`. Keep the `snapshot_id`. The first time in a session,
   screenshot too, so you know what you started from.
2. **Run** the script.
3. **After, structure:** `summer_snapshot_diff` with `from_id` set to the id you kept. Omit
   `to_id` and the engine snapshots the current state and diffs against your baseline.
4. **After, pixels:** `summer_screenshot`, and look at it.
5. Fix before stacking more work on a broken base. An empty diff after a "successful"
   mutation is a red flag, not a success.

## World snapshots

`summer_world_snapshot` (the `GetWorldSnapshot` operation) is a compact, deterministic read of
the edited scene built to be diffed:

- Every node carries `path`, `class`, `name`, `visible`, and for 3D nodes `pos`, `rot_deg`,
  `scale` and a scene-root-relative world `aabb`. Instanced scenes carry `scene_file`.
- Resources are **fingerprinted, not dumped**: `script_fp` and `material_fps` are short
  checksums that change when the resource changes. They tell you *that* something changed,
  never what it contains.
- Floats are rounded to three decimals and nodes are sorted by path, so two snapshots of an
  unchanged scene are identical and a diff has no phantom changes.
- Caps are explicit: 4000 nodes and 20 lights by default, with `truncated`,
  `lights_truncated` and per-node `material_fps_truncated` flags. A capped list is never
  presented as complete. 2D nodes are included only when asked (`include_2d`).
- The result also summarizes `lights` (path, class, energy, color), `cameras` (path and the
  stored `current` flag), an `environment_fp`, and `counts` by class.

The engine keeps the last eight snapshots per session. Diffing against an id that has expired
returns `failure_reason: "unknown_snapshot"`; take a fresh baseline and redo the pair.

## Snapshot diffs

`summer_snapshot_diff` (`DiffWorldSnapshot`) returns `added`, `removed` and `changed` node
paths, a `counts_delta` by class, `environment_changed` when the environment fingerprint
moved, and a `truncation_warning` if either snapshot was capped. For `changed` entries the
`fields` list names what moved: class, position, rotation, scale, bounds, visibility, script
or material fingerprint, scene file.

Read the receipt against your intent, not against "did anything happen":

- `added` is exactly what you meant to add — no more, no less.
- `removed` is empty unless you deleted on purpose. A node you created appearing here, or
  missing from `added` after a save, is the ownership bug explained in
  [Concepts](/guides/scripting/concepts#ownership-and-saving).
- `changed` names only nodes you touched. Anything else means your script had side effects.

A snapshot's world bounds also make physical plausibility checkable: nothing floats above or
sinks into its support, neighbours do not interpenetrate, and sizes are sane — a door around
two units, a person around 1.7, a car around 4.5. A prop with a 40-unit bound is an import
scale problem, not a placement problem.

## Screenshots, and which framing to trust

`summer_screenshot` has three targets and, for scene renders, a set of framings:

| Question | Call |
|---|---|
| How does the open tab look right now? | `target: "viewport"` (the default) |
| Is the composition and scale of a scene file right? | `target: "scene"` with a preset framing (`iso`, `top`, `front`, `back`, `left`, `right`) and optionally `scenePath` |
| Is one node placed right? | `target: "scene"` with `nodePath` — the camera fits that node's bounds; an unresolvable path fails with `node_not_found` rather than silently framing the whole scene |
| Is the **lighting, mood or environment** right? | `target: "scene"` with `framing: "camera"` |
| What does the running game show? | `target: "game"` (after `summer_play`) |

`framing: "camera"` is the framing that tells the truth about light. It renders through the
scene's own current (or first) `Camera3D`, or the one you name with `camera_path`, with the
scene's **real** `WorldEnvironment` and no synthetic light injected. The preset framings
substitute an environment so that an unlit scene is still visible, which makes them useless
for judging emissive materials, sky, fog or exposure. The result confesses which is which:
`used_scene_camera`, `camera_path`, and `environment_used` with one of
`scene_world_environment`, `none_default_clear_color` or `synthetic_preview_environment`.
A scene with no camera fails camera framing with `no_scene_camera`; a wrong `camera_path`
with `camera_not_found`; combining `nodePath` with camera framing is `node_focus_unsupported`.

Preset renders are static (time zero), synthetic-camera, substitute-environment images. Do
not draw conclusions about animation, particles, lighting or mood from them.

## Runtime reads during a playtest

The edited scene is not the running game. Spawned enemies, pooled projectiles, autoloads and
runtime-built UI exist only while the game runs, and often at different paths.

- `summer_get_runtime_tree` (`GetRuntimeSceneTree`) reads the live tree — a subtree by
  `path`, to a `depth` (default 3) and `limit` (default 500), with `total_nodes` and
  `truncated` so a cap is never silent. Paths are absolute runtime paths such as
  `/root/Main/Player`, and the result is stamped `provenance.source: "runtime"` so it can
  never be mistaken for an editor read.
- `summer_inspect_runtime_node` (`GetRuntimeNode`) reads one node's live properties — actual
  position, actual stats, actual flags.

Inspect live rather than stopping the game: stopping usually resets the state you are chasing.
Without a running game both return `failure_reason: "game_not_running"`; start one with
`summer_play` and re-run. Other failures name themselves: `node_not_found`, `timeout`,
`request_failed` when no debug session is up yet.

## Reading the run result itself

Before any of the above, the script's own result is evidence:

- `ok: false` with `failure_reason` and `error` — nothing to verify yet; read the message.
- `ok: true` but `errors` non-empty — should not happen for a clean run; treat it as a
  failure.
- `reports.prop_warnings` non-empty — a property name was ignored. The scene is not what you
  asked for even though the run "succeeded".
- Helper report entries (`make_shader_errors`, `unowned_nodes`, `decimate`, …) — each one is
  a helper telling you it did less than asked, or something different, and why.
- `rolled_back: true` — the scene is back to its pre-run state; fix and re-run.
- `undo_action: "unavailable"`, `no_rewind_point: true`, `files_written`,
  `project_settings_changed` — your safety nets have changed shape; note it before the next
  run.

## Honesty rules

These pages are read by agents that act on them without a human's skepticism, so the rules are
blunt:

- **Claim only what a diff, a frame or a diagnostic proved, and cite it.** "The diff shows 25
  boxes added; the camera-framing screenshot shows them lit on the terrain." Not "the grid
  looks great."
- **Never describe an image you did not receive.** A failed capture is a result — report it,
  fall back a level (scene render to viewport), or ask.
- **Pass structured failures through verbatim.** `failure_reason`, `terminalState`,
  `rolled_back: false` with a `rollback_error` — not "it didn't quite work".
- **Verify by the effect, not by the value you just set.** Reading back a property you wrote
  proves nothing; the reloaded scene having the node, the diff showing it, the frame showing
  it — those do.
- **Do not compound on an unverified base.** Two mutations without a diff or screenshot
  between them means the second one is building on guesswork.
