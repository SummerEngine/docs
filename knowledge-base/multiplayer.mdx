---
title: "Does Summer support multiplayer games?"
description: "Yes. Summer runs a server for every match, finds players through matchmaking, and lets you test everything on your own machine."
icon: "question"
---

**Yes.** A Summer multiplayer game is one project with a client that every player runs
and a server that owns the rules. When you publish, Summer runs a server for every match
and brings players together through matchmaking. With Local Play you can test the whole
game, server and several players, on your own computer first.

## What you get

- **Servers for every match**, run by Summer. You choose the size, and you don't need to
  rent or manage any machines yourself.
- **Matchmaking**, from quick play to ranked and fixed teams, plus parties so friends play
  together.
- **Movement that feels instant** for each player, and hit checks that are fair to
  everyone.
- **Saves**: player progress, and worlds that keep running between sessions.
- **Leaderboards, chat, friends and a store** that your game can use directly.

Start with [Make your game multiplayer](/build/multiplayer), then pick the pieces your
game needs from the [Build your game](/build/multiplayer#where-to-go-next) guides.

## Your agent can build it

Each Build guide has prompts you can copy into your coding agent. The agent reads your
project and uses Summer's published skills to add the feature, then tests it with Local
Play.

## Bringing your own server

Summer Engine also includes the standard multiplayer API (`MultiplayerPeer`,
`MultiplayerSpawner`, `MultiplayerSynchronizer`, `@rpc`) with ENet and WebSocket
transports, for games you host on your own infrastructure.
