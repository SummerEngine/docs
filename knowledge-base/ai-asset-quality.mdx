## Made to ship

Summer Engine generates the assets your game needs: 3D models, rigged characters, textures, sprites and audio, in styles from realistic to pixel art. They go straight into your game, and you can refine them or swap in your own art at any time.

## What AI Assets Do Well

**Rapid concept exploration.** Try different visual directions without commissioning art. Generate a low-poly tree, a cartoon character, a pixel-art sword. See what fits your game.

**Props and environments at scale.** Crates, barrels, trees, floors, whole sets. Generate them in seconds and fill your world.

**Quick iteration on visual ideas.** "Make it more weathered." "Add a glow effect." "Change the color to blue." Refine in real time.

**Indie game aesthetics.** Many successful indie games use a cohesive AI-assisted or stylized look. Low-poly, pixel art, and cartoon styles work well.

**Specific use cases.** Character sprites, environment backgrounds, UI elements, concept art and mood boards.

## What AI Assets Are Not Good For

**Final AAA production art.** Big-budget games need art that matches exact specs, passes legal review, and meets studio quality bars. AI assets are not there yet.

**Specific, highly detailed character designs.** "A knight with a scar over his left eye, wearing rusted armor, holding a broken shield." AI may get close but often misses fine details. Human artists excel here.

**Brand-consistent visual style across many assets.** Maintaining exact style across 100+ assets is hard for AI. Human art direction is better.

**High-quality promotional materials.** Key art, trailers, store assets. These need human polish.

## The Workflow

Use AI assets to prototype and iterate. AI is a tool for speed and iteration; human artists bring polish and specificity. When you are ready to ship, replace key assets with human-made art if your budget allows. Many indies ship with a mix: AI for props and environments, humans for main characters and key art.
