Publishing on Summer Games is free. You make a store page in Studio, upload your build and submit it. Summer staff review it, and then your game goes live on [summer.games](https://summer.games).

Start at [summerengine.com/studio/upload](https://summerengine.com/studio/upload), or click **Publish a game** in Grow. You can fill in the first step before you sign in; Studio keeps your draft and asks you to sign in when you save.

<Tip>
  Want your AI agent to do every step? It fills the store page, makes the art, uploads the build and submits, then stops at one **Approve publishing** click for you. See [Create and publish your game](/publishing/summer-games).
</Tip>

## The steps

Studio walks you through one step at a time. You can go back to any step.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Game and targets">
    Give your game a **title** (up to 60 characters) and choose its **targets**: Web, macOS, Windows, Linux, iPhone and Android. Targets decide which store pages you fill next:

    - **Web, macOS, Windows or Linux** open the **Desktop store**: summer.games and the Summer Games desktop app.
    - **iPhone or Android** open the **Mobile store**: the Summer Games apps for iPhone and Android.

    You can add a **build file** here too, or later. If a jam is open, you can enter it here: see [Summer Games Jam](/grow/game-jam). Click **Save and continue**. This makes a private draft of your store page. It does not submit anything.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Desktop store">
    The text and art for summer.games and the desktop app, with a live preview next to the form: a tagline (up to 80 characters), a description (120-300 characters), 3-8 tags, key art, 4-8 screenshots and an icon. A trailer and capsule art are optional. See [Store page text](/publishing/store-text) and [Store art](/publishing/store-art).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mobile store">
    The same for the phone apps, with a phone preview: a shorter description (120-170 characters), key art, 3-8 screenshots and an icon. **Same as desktop** copies your desktop text once; **Use desktop icon** reuses the icon.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get paid">
    Choose free or paid. A paid game needs a price and a connected Stripe account; free games work without one. See [Earnings & payouts](/grow/earnings).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check and submit">
    The **Before launch** checklist shows what is still needed to go live and what makes the page shine. Each row links to its fix. When nothing required is left, click **Submit for review**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Live">
    Where your game is on its way to players: in review, approved, or live.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The full dashboard of a game (**Full dashboard** on its page, in **Your games**) has every field in tabs: **Store page**, **Audience** (platforms, content classification, languages, system requirements, release date), **Builds** and **Sales**.

## Your build: browser play or downloads

| Players get | Upload | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **Play in the browser** on summer.games | A **web export**, zipped, under **Play in the browser** on the game's page `index.html` at the root of the zip, not in a folder. Up to 500 MB, 2,000 files and 200 MB per file. See [web exports](#web-exports) below. |
| **A download** for Mac, Windows or Linux | One file per platform | Up to 40 GB in Studio. |
| **The Summer Games apps** on iPhone and Android | A Summer export from the editor | Checked by Summer for each platform. See [Export your game](/publishing/export). |

- A **Summer export** is checked in Studio and made ready for each platform. It asks for a version, such as `v1.0.0`.
- **Any other build** (`.zip`, `.exe`, `.dmg`, `.apk` or `.ipa`) stays private, and Summer staff check it during review.
- Without a web export, players download your game instead of playing it in the browser.
- Games that use C# (.NET) need a download per platform; they do not run in the browser or the Summer Games apps.

Large uploads resume if they stop. Keep the tab open until the upload finishes.

### Web exports

summer.games plays any web export with `index.html` at the root of its `.zip`:

- **Godot's standard Web export** uses the Compatibility renderer (WebGL 2). Export to Web, zip the contents of the export folder, and upload it.
- **Summer's web export** (`summer_export_game`, format `download`, target `web`) runs Forward+ or Mobile projects on WebGPU. See [Export your game](/publishing/export).
- **Thread support off** plays on the game page. A build with threads opens in its own tab.
- Other engines' web builds work the same way.

## Review and going live

| Status | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Draft** | Not submitted. Saving never submits. |
| **In review** | Summer staff are reviewing your page and build. You can edit again once the review is done. |
| **Changes requested** / **Not approved** | The review found something to change. Change it and submit again. |
| **Approved** | Click **Go live** to put the page on Summer Games. |
| **Live** | Your page is on summer.games. |

A live store page and a released version are two things:

- A **web export** and **downloads** go to players as soon as the page is live.
- A **Summer export** goes out when you pick it under **Versions** and click **Promote to production**. Uploading a new version never replaces the live one by itself.

Editing a live page starts a new draft, which goes through review before players see it.

<Tip>
  **Share a preview.** On the store page, **Share preview** makes a private link that expires in 1 hour, 1 day or 7 days. Anyone with the link sees your saved page before it is on Summer Games.
</Tip>

## With your agent

In Studio, ask the assistant "publish my game". It opens **Publish a game**, fills the title and targets, and walks the store page with you. It never presses **Submit for review** unless you ask for exactly that. See [Use the agent](/grow/use-the-agent).

From Claude, Codex or ChatGPT, the Summer Engine MCP does every step and gives you an approval link. See [Approve publishing](/publishing/owner-approval).
